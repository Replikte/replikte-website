What Nora can do

Five things the live demo above and the cabinet walkthrough below are designed to show — not generic AI answers, but work tied to your operation, your files, and your day.

Knows your day

Nora reads what you have connected: calendar, open work, customer threads, and the routines we set up during your engagement. In the morning you can ask what's next the same way you'd ask an ops person — and get an ordered list, not a wall of notifications.

She surfaces who needs a follow-up, what meeting to prep for, and when to nudge someone you said you'd get back to. Sensitive sends still go through you: drafts and reminders, approve before anything goes out.

Takes a real brief

You do not have to translate your intent into prompts. Send a blueprint, a reference photo, a PDF, or plain-English notes about what to match — in Telegram, like any other message.

For cabinetry and built-ins, Nora can run a cabinet designer against your room layout and style reference: wall positions from the plan, door proportions and lighting from the photo, constraints from your text. The brief stays attached to the thread so nothing lives only in your head.

Produces a client-ready concept

The output is a render you could show a homeowner the same evening — not a stock moodboard, not a generic “inspiration” grid. It is tied to their room geometry and the style you pointed at.

That matters when the sale is visual and the clock is ticking: showroom visits, revision calls, and “can you show me something?” before the competitor does.

Checks her own work

After a concept lands, Nora compares it to the blueprint and reference — layout match, style match, and where things drifted. You get scores and a short read in the thread, not a silent image dump.

If the fireplace wall or TV recess is off, she can run a second pass with your corrections instead of you discovering it in front of the client.

Knows what she isn't

Every render is labeled as a concept visualization — not as-built, not permit-ready, not code-compliant. Nora does not pretend to be your drafter or your engineer.

The same discipline applies to email, calendar, and money: approval-first on anything that leaves the building. The conversation is easy; the guardrails are deliberate.

Cabinet designer — example thread

A morning in Telegram

Same thread as the hero — replay it here with the step-by-step breakdown. Placeholder names; real workflow. More cabinet examples can sit alongside this layout later.

1. Day brief — agenda, follow-ups, scheduled nudge.

2. Brief — blueprint for layout, photo for door style and brass niche lighting, notes on elevation D.

3. Concept — first pass with layout/style scores against the plan.

4. Refinement — second pass with central fireplace and higher layout match.

5. Next steps — Nora suggests actions (proposal draft, client email, another pass) as Telegram-style buttons.

Throughout: concept-only labeling so nothing is mistaken for construction documents.

Under the hood

Same org, same context, one surface

Nora runs in Telegram but is tied to your Replikte organization: company profile, timezone, contacts, and the tools your admin connected — typically Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Drive for approved company files.

Skill packs (for example residential cabinetry) keep guidance grounded in how you sell and deliver, without inventing pricing, permits, or code. Work items and recurring check-ins live in the same system, so a thread with a homeowner stays linked to the job, not lost in a camera roll.

The Replikte team builds and tunes the wiring during the engagement. Nora is how you and your team use it between site visits — voice notes, photos, and PDFs included.

What this isn't

Nora is not a product you buy off a website and set up alone on a Sunday. There is no free tier and no solo onboarding wizard.

It works because we spend the first weeks learning how your business runs, then connect the right tools, routines, and approvals to a surface your team already knows. See how Nora fits the engagement on the home page.
