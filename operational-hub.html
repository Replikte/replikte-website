From paper tickets to one operational hub.

For an owner-operated field company that is evaluating a large system. We fit the technology to the business, not the reverse. Digitize the work where it happens, keep one record, and connect to the tools already in use. A larger system can wait until real usage says what it should be.

The record lives in the truck.

Paper job tickets, a whiteboard, a shared calendar, a spreadsheet of devices, an accounting package, and photos on someone's phone. The work gets done. The record of the work does not survive the drive back.

  • Tickets come back late, wet, or unreadable, and the work never gets billed.
  • Invoices wait one to three weeks because someone has to retype the ticket.
  • Compliance certificates are typed into PDFs after the fact, or missed.
  • Dispatch finds people by calling them.
  • The next visit starts from memory.

A large system on day one asks the company to change how it works. This approach starts with the ticket the technician already fills out.

Owner-operated field companies.

This is for you if:

  • The work is done in the field and the record of it is still on paper, in a spreadsheet, or in someone's head
  • Invoices wait on a ticket that has to be retyped
  • You have repeat visits, inspections, or certificates that have to be filed
  • You are being sold a large system and want to see the highest-win slice working first

This is not a fit if:

  • You want a full system replacement on day one
  • You need a live accounting integration in the first cycle. The first cycle hands work to the system you already run
  • You are looking for a published case study of a named client

How an engagement runs.

Discovery with the people who do the work. A single use case chosen for impact and effort. An MVP in two-week cycles, tested and signed off by your team. After the first cycle, two lanes ship together: new work, and improvements to what is already live. Feedback from the people using it re-ranks what comes next.

Accounting, calendar, and the other tools you already pay for stay. We connect to them. We do not ask the company to move house on day one.

Walk your own tickets through it.

Bring two recent jobs and one that fell through the cracks. We'll show where a first cycle would start, and what we would leave alone.

Common questions.

Does this replace our accounting system?

No. The first cycle hands approved work to the accounting system you already use. A live connection is not required to start.

Why not start with an ERP?

An ERP can be the right later decision, once you know how the work actually moves. The first cycle proves the highest-win slice — a technician closes a job, and the office has an invoice-ready record the same day — and later cycles grow from what the team uses.

What is a cycle?

Two weeks. Cycle 1 is the MVP. After that, Lane A ships something new and Lane B improves what is already live, and they release together. “With you” on a release means your team tested it and signed off.
