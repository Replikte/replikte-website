Field service · Agentic system

From paper tickets to one operational hub.

For an owner-operated field company that is evaluating a large system. We fit the technology to the business, not the reverse. Digitize the work where it happens, keep one record, and connect to the tools already in use. A larger system can wait until real usage says what it should be.

The walkthrough further down uses a fictional company. Every number in it is sample data. It runs in the browser. No login, no accounts.

Walk the five-minute demo

The record lives in the truck.

Paper job tickets, a whiteboard, a shared calendar, a spreadsheet of devices, an accounting package, and photos on someone's phone. The work gets done. The record of the work does not survive the drive back.

  • Tickets come back late, wet, or unreadable, and the work never gets billed.
  • Invoices wait one to three weeks because someone has to retype the ticket.
  • Compliance certificates are typed into PDFs after the fact, or missed.
  • Dispatch finds people by calling them.
  • The next visit starts from memory.

A large system on day one asks the company to change how it works. This approach starts with the ticket the technician already fills out.

The demo

Ridgeline Plumbing & Backflow is fictional. About 45 people in the story: an owner, an office, a bookkeeper, foremen, and field technicians. Use the role switcher. There is no login.

Presenter script (about five minutes)
  1. Discovery (45 s). “Before we touched software, we spent time with the crews and the office. Here's where information got lost and what it was costing.” Open How we got here → Discovery.
  2. Roadmap (20 s). “We picked the one use case with the biggest win for the least effort: tickets to invoice-ready.” Open Roadmap.
  3. Technician (60 s). “This is the tech on site. Readings, parts, photos, signature, done. No paper.” Switch to Technician. Open Kettlehouse Diner. Fill readings, add a part and a photo, sign, and close.
  4. Office (45 s). “Same day, the office reviews it. One click, and the certificate is generated.” Switch to Office. Approve. Download the certificate.
  5. Bookkeeper (20 s). “It lands invoice-ready and exports to the accounting system they already use.” Switch to Bookkeeper. Export for your accounting system.
  6. Owner (30 s). “The owner finally sees what's finished, what's unbilled, and what retests are coming.” Switch to Owner.
  7. Cycles (60 s). Toggle Cycle 2 and Cycle 3. “Every two weeks, two lanes ship together. New features, plus improvements the team asked for. Their feedback re-ranks the roadmap.”
  8. Close (20 s). “That's the approach: fit the technology to how the business works, prove it on the highest win, then grow it in priced phases.”

Owner-operated field companies.

This is for you if:

  • The work is done in the field and the record of it is still on paper, in a spreadsheet, or in someone's head
  • Invoices wait on a ticket that has to be retyped
  • You have repeat visits, inspections, or certificates that have to be filed
  • You are being sold a large system and want to see the highest-win slice working first

This is not a fit if:

  • You want a full system replacement on day one
  • You need a live accounting integration in the first cycle — the demo exports a CSV into the system you already run
  • You are looking for a case study of a named client — Ridgeline is fictional, and the numbers are sample data

How an engagement runs.

Discovery with the people who do the work. A single use case chosen for impact and effort. An MVP in two-week cycles, tested and signed off by your team. After the first cycle, two lanes ship together: new work, and improvements to what is already live. Feedback from the people using it re-ranks what comes next.

Accounting, calendar, and the other tools you already pay for stay. We connect to them. We do not ask the company to move house on day one.

Common questions.

Is Ridgeline a real client?

No. Ridgeline Plumbing & Backflow, the people, the sites, and the jobs are fictional. The demo exists so a prospect can click through the method. It is not a case study.

Are the hours and dollars real results?

No. Every metric and chart is labeled Sample data. The “before” figure for days-to-invoice is illustrative. Nothing on this page is a measured client outcome.

Does this replace our accounting system?

No. Approved jobs export as a CSV labeled for the accounting system you already use. There is no live connection in the demo, and the first cycle does not require one.

Why not start with an ERP?

An ERP can be the right later decision, once you know how the work actually moves. The first cycle proves the highest-win slice — a technician closes a job, and the office has an invoice-ready record the same day — and later cycles grow from what the team uses.

What is a cycle?

Two weeks. Cycle 1 is the MVP. After that, Lane A ships something new and Lane B improves what is already live, and they release together. “With you” on a release means your team tested it and signed off.

What if a test fails?

The screen says Fail in words, not only in color. The job moves to Needs follow-up and stays out of the invoice queue until someone schedules the return.
